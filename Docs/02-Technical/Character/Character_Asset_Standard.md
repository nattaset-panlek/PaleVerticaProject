# Character Asset Standard

Status: **draft 2026-10-17**. Verified against Unity 6000.4.8f1, PSD Importer 13.0.3, 2D Animation 14.0.4.

Applies to every character body PSB and every costume PSB that swaps body parts. The goal: a new character, drawn by hand or generated by an AI, imports into Unity and works in the shared rig with no manual fixing.

Step-by-step use of the tools: [Character Asset Walkthrough](./Character_Asset_Walkthrough.md). Evidence and measurements: [Character Asset Analysis](./Character_Asset_Analysis.md). Prompts for image AIs: [Character AI Art Prompts](./Character_AI_Art_Prompts.md). Template files: [Template/](./Template/).

---

## 1. How a character is assembled

```text
PSB (6 part layers on a 400 x 400 canvas)
   |  PSD Importer: one sprite per layer, packed into a mosaic atlas
   v
Sprites "Head", "Body", "ArmL", "ArmR", "LegL", "LegR"   (pivot = rig anchor)
   |  swapped into the shared prefab rig (copy of Skeletom.prefab)
   v
Model (Animator: Model.controller, cutout clips Idle/walk/attack/die/Awake)
  LegR/LegR  LegL/LegL  Body/Body  Body/ArmR/ArmR  Body/Head/Head  Body/ArmL/ArmL   Shadow
```

Three things form the contract. Break one and the character breaks:

1. **Layer names**: sprites are found by name (prefab tool, costume system).
2. **Canvas positions**: the rig's joints sit at fixed canvas points (section 4).
3. **Pivots on the rig anchors**: each sprite's pivot must land on its anchor, so a part of any size sits where it was drawn.

There are no bones, weights, `SpriteSkin` or Sprite Library categories. Animation rotates and moves the joint `Transform`s.

## 2. File rules

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Format | `.psb`. A PSD (version 1) saved by any app may be renamed to `.psb`; Unity reads the version from the file header |
| Location | `Assets/Image/Character/<CharacterName>.psb` |
| Canvas | **400 x 400 px**, transparent background |
| Color | RGB, 8 bits per channel |
| Part layers | Plain pixel layers at the **root** (not inside groups), Normal blend, 100% opacity, visible |
| Forbidden on parts | Layer masks, clipping masks, layer effects (stroke, shadow), adjustment layers, smart objects, text layers. Merge or rasterize them into the part first |
| Helper layers | Anything else (sketches, guides, references, backgrounds) must be **hidden** and its name must start with `_` (`_Guide`, `_Ref_...`). Hidden layers are never imported |

## 3. Layers

### 3.1 Required layers, layer panel top to bottom

The panel order equals the in-game draw order, so the PSB preview looks like the game.

| # | Layer name | Sorting order in prefab | Joint (rotation point) | Must cover with opaque pixels |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `ArmL` | 11 | shoulder L (245, 149) | its own shoulder; the body covers it too |
| 2 | `Head` | 10 | neck (207, 168) | the neck point, at least 8 px below it |
| 3 | `Body` | 9 | torso root (207, 126) | both shoulders and both hips |
| 4 | `ArmR` | 8 | shoulder R (172, 149) | its own shoulder; the body covers it too |
| 5 | `LegL` | 7 | hip L (228, 83) | its own hip, at least 8 px above it |
| 6 | `LegR` | 6 | hip R (175, 83) | its own hip, at least 8 px above it |
| 7 | `_Guide` (optional) | n/a | hidden group | n/a |

Coordinates are canvas pixels with **y from the bottom** (Unity). In Photoshop or Krita (y from the top) use `400 - y`: the shoulders are at y 251, the neck at 232, the hips at 317.

### 3.2 Naming rules

- Exact spelling and case: `Head`, not `head`, `HEAD`, `Head copy` or `Layer 5`.
- Each part name appears **once in the whole file**, hidden layers included. Reference copies are named `_Ref_<Part>`.
- `L` and `R` are the **character's** own left and right as it faces the camera: `ArmL` and `LegL` are on the **right side of the image**. With the head turned slightly to the image's left, that side is nearer the camera, so it draws in front.
- After a PSB has been imported, **never rename, delete or re-create a part layer**. The importer matches layers to sprites; a lost match creates a new sprite ID and every prefab that used the old sprite shows nothing.
- Layer IDs: Photoshop stores a unique ID per layer and the importer maps by it (`Layer Mapping: Use Layer ID`). Apps that store no IDs still import, with a warning, by falling back to names. The three reference PSBs had no IDs; real IDs equal to their old mapping were added on 2026-10-17.

## 4. Canvas, scale and pivots

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Canvas | 400 x 400 px. Tall headwear may use a taller canvas, up to **400 x 480**, with the extra height added **above** only (Photoshop: Canvas Size, anchor bottom center). Every coordinate here is measured from the bottom, so joints, anchors and the ground stay the same. Used by the Alchemist and Shopkeeper (400 x 440) |
| Pixels Per Unit | 100 |
| Prefab scale | the rig scales parts by 0.23428594, so 1 canvas px = 0.00234 world units and a full character (about 370 px) is about 0.87 units tall |
| Ground (feet) | y = 22 (tolerance 14 px) |
| Center line | x = 207 |
| Character origin | (207, 168): the `Model` root, collider center |
| Shadow | centered at (208, 23) |
| Document pivot | Bottom Center (only used by the importer's own preview prefab) |

### 4.1 Rig anchors

Each sprite's pivot is set to its **anchor**, a fixed canvas point measured from the shared prefab (it equals Acrxer's part centers). The tool computes the normalized pivot from the layer's position and size:

$$pivot = \frac{anchor - layerPosition}{layerSize}$$

| Part | Anchor (x, y) | Joint (x, y) | Reference box x0-x1, y0-y1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ArmL | 274.49, 128.02 | 245.38, 149.20 | 232-317, 92-164 |
| Head | 206.93, 268.54 | 206.97, 168.40 | 62-352, 144-393 |
| Body | 206.50, 127.00 | 206.97, 125.72 | 149-264, 59-195 |
| ArmR | 139.49, 128.02 | 171.63, 149.20 | 97-182, 92-164 |
| LegL | 235.48, 57.00 | 228.31, 83.04 | 213-258, 22-92 |
| LegR | 175.38, 57.00 | 174.96, 83.04 | 153-198, 22-92 |

The reference boxes are Acrxer's sizes. Parts may be bigger or smaller; the joints may not move.

## 5. Drawing rules

- **One part per layer.** Never merge two limbs, never draw an arm on the body layer, never put both arms on one layer.
- **Overlap at every joint.** Paint the hidden ends completely (no holes, no missing outline under the body): they become visible when a part rotates.
  - Arms: the shoulder end reaches past the shoulder joint toward the body by at least 8 px (12-20 px is safer).
  - Legs: the top reaches above the hip joint, under the body, by at least 8 px (15-25 px is safer).
  - Head: the chin area reaches below the neck joint, over the body, by at least 8 px (15-25 px is safer).
  - Body: covers both shoulder and both hip joints.
  - The minimums are what the three reference characters have; the validator checks that the joint pixel itself is opaque.
- **Pose**: neutral standing pose. Body nearly front-facing, head and face turned slightly toward the **left of the image** (the game shows the art as drawn when walking left and mirrors `Model` with `scale.x = -1` when walking right, `CharacterPresenter`). Head upright, no head tilt or neck lean. Arms hanging down and slightly outward, legs straight, feet on y = 22.
- **Face**: minimal (dot or closed-line eyes, tiny mouth, blush); no nose, teeth or lip detail.
- **Silhouette**: chibi proportions. The head (with hair or hat) is about 55-65% of the total height and wider than the body. Hats belong to the `Head` layer.
- **Detail**: low. Hands are round nubs with no fingers, feet are rounded stubs with no toes or shoe details, clothes are big flat shapes with at most one or two small details. The whole character is small on screen.
- **Edges**: clean alpha, no stray pixels, no glow or soft shadow beyond the outline (each would grow the sprite rect and the atlas).
- **Style**: see [Character AI Art Prompts](./Character_AI_Art_Prompts.md), section 1.

### 6.1 Rig and Sprite Library

- **No skinning**: no bones, weights or geometry in the Skinning Editor. The prefab rig is a cutout `Transform` hierarchy; skinning data would be ignored.
- **No Sprite Library categories**: costumes swap sprites by name (`CharacterVisualController`), not through `SpriteResolver`.
- Moving to skeletal animation or Sprite Library swapping is a separate design change; update this standard first if that happens.

## 7. Prefab contract

A character prefab is a copy of `Assets/Addressable/Prefab/Entity/Enemy/Skeletom.prefab` (the tool makes it). Keep these paths and names: the animation clips bind to them.

| Path under the root | Holds | Sorting order |
|---|---|---|
| `Model` | `Animator` (Model.controller), `SortingGroup` (Default, order 6) | |
| `Model/LegR/LegR`, `Model/LegL/LegL` | leg sprites | 6, 7 |
| `Model/Body/Body` | body sprite | 9 |
| `Model/Body/ArmR/ArmR`, `Model/Body/ArmL/ArmL` | arm sprites | 8, 11 |
| `Model/Body/Head/Head` | head sprite | 10 |
| `Model/Shadow` | shadow sprite, `SortingGroup` with **Sort At Root** (Default, order 1) | 1 |

Do not move the joint objects (`Model/Body`, `Model/Body/ArmL`, ...): they are the rig. Art adapts to the rig, not the other way round.

**Sorting.** The part orders are local to the character's `Model` group, so each character draws as one unit and two characters never interleave. Characters sort front to back by Y (lower on screen draws in front): `Assets/Settings/Renderer2D.asset` uses Transparency Sort Mode **Custom Axis** (0, 1, 0). The shadow's own group sorts at the root, so every shadow draws under every character while staying at `Model/Shadow` (the clips animate it there). Keep both groups on `Model`, not on the root: Time Dilation ghosts clone `Model`, and `BuffCanvas` must stay outside the group.

**Characters placed straight in a scene** (not a prefab instance, for example the unpacked `Player` rig in `SandBox.unity`) do not get these groups from the prefabs. Without them each part sorts on its own order (6 to 11), so the body always draws over MapLab walls and over other characters at order 6. Add them with **Character Sorting Scene Installer** (section 9). Check: select `Player/Model` in the Inspector, it has a Sorting Group with order 6, and `Player/Model/Shadow` has one with order 1 and Sort At Root ticked. In Play mode the player walks behind a wall above it and in front of a wall below it.

## 8. Atlas distortion: cause and fix

When a layer is redrawn and its size changes, the PSD Importer (with Automatic Reslice off) **keeps the old sprite rect size** and only moves it. The sprite then shows a crop of the new layer or extra padding around it. Measured on 2026-10-17:

| PSB | Head layer | Head sprite rect | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Skeleton | 254 x 216 | 248 x 209 | 6 px cut off the right, 7 px off the top |
| zombie | 255 x 222 | 259 x 218 | 4 px cut off the top |
| Acrxer | 290 x 249 | 291 x 251 | 1-2 px of padding included, pivot off by 1 px |

**Rebuild Atlas** turns Automatic Reslice on, raises the padding to 8 px and reimports. Sprite IDs stay the same, so prefab references survive. With reslice on, the problem cannot come back.

## 10. Checklist: new character

**Artist (or AI)**

1. Open `Character_Template.psd`. Turn `_Guide` on while drawing.
2. Draw each part on its own layer over the blocking shape. Keep every joint (magenta ring) inside its part and inside the body (section 5).
3. Keep the six layer names and their order. Delete or hide the blocking colors you painted over; do not rename layers.
4. Hide `_Guide` and every other helper layer. Their names start with `_`.
5. Save as `<CharacterName>.psb` (or save PSD and rename to `.psb`) at 400 x 400.

**Unity**

6. Put the file in `Assets/Image/Character/`.
7. Open **Pale Vertica > Character > Character Asset Tool**, select the PSB, press **Validate**.
8. Fix every error in the art file (missing or misnamed layers, uncovered joints). Save; Unity reimports.
9. Press **Apply Standard**, then **Validate** again until there are no errors.
10. Press **Create Prefab From Base**.
11. In **Window > Pale Vertica > Pale Vertica GameDataWorkbench** add the character to `Character.json` (`PrefabPath` = the new prefab path) and set stats. Prefabs under `Assets/Addressable/Prefab/` are Addressable through the folder entry in the `Prefab` group; their address is their path.
12. Play mode: check idle, walk, attack and die; joints must not open gaps.

**Costume PSB**: same canvas, layers and anchors, with only the parts it replaces. Run Apply Standard so its sprites share the anchors.

## 11. Reference PSBs after 2026-10-17

| PSB | Layers | Atlas | Pivots | Validator warnings to expect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Acrxer | standard | stale head rect until Rebuild Atlas | Center (equal to the anchors once the head rect is rebuilt) | padding, reslice, Head pivot 1.1 px off |
| Skeleton | standard (`Layer 5` renamed `Head`) | head cropped until Rebuild Atlas | Center (up to 12 px off anchors) | padding, reslice, pivots |
| zombie | standard (moved out of `Folder 8`) | head cropped until Rebuild Atlas | Center (up to 10 px off anchors) | padding, reslice, pivots |

Run **Rebuild Atlas** on all three to fix the cropping. **Apply Standard** also moves their parts onto the anchors. `Ally`, `Dummy` and `Skeletom` (and `SandBox.unity`) use Skeleton, so their parts will shift by up to 12 px to the positions drawn in the PSB. That is a visible change; do it deliberately.

## 12. Not verified inside Unity

These were written while the Unity editor was open, so nothing here ran inside Unity:
- The editor scripts compile against Unity 6000.4.8f1 and the project's assemblies (checked with Unity's Roslyn compiler), but none of the buttons has been run.
- The three edited PSBs were checked with psd-tools (pixels and bounds identical, layer IDs set). Unity's reimport has not been observed. After it, the prefabs should still show their sprites; if any part is missing, restore the PSB from git or from the backup noted in the analysis doc.
